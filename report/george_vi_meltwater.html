How we know the detections are real

Validation

The shelf total is dominated by one tile (19DEA), so it was checked on its own with 80 stratified blind reference points — labelled by eye without seeing the algorithm's answer, then scored against a hidden key.

Method, in four moves

Reproducible · free data
01

Spectral detection

NDWI (green–NIR) > 0.19 with a green-minus-red shadow test and hysteresis growth — the published Moussavi et al. 2020 Antarctic-lake method.

02

Authoritative boundary

Detection is masked to the MEaSUReS Antarctic Boundaries v2 shelf polygon, so mountains and open ocean are excluded and only the floating shelf counts.

03

One fixed grid

Eleven Sentinel-2 tiles across three UTM zones are reprojected onto a single EPSG:3031 grid and unioned — a constant denominator every season.

04

Peak-melt selection

Each tile contributes its highest-melt date in the Dec–Feb window (ranked cheaply at 60 m), capturing the true seasonal maximum rather than the clearest sky.

Honest limits. Absolute areas are provisional: cloudier seasons (flagged above) rest on hazier scenes, and the seasonal maximum is a single peak date per tile, not a full multi-date composite. The relative ranking of melt years is robust; the absolute km² carries roughly a 20–25% uncertainty.
